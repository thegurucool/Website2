Malaysia's AI Education Plans for 2026: What's Coming, and What Schools Can Do Now

Jul 30, 2026  ·  By May Yang

Malaysia is advancing AI in education through three initiatives moving in parallel in 2026: the National Education Plan 2026-2035, launched in January, a National AI Action Plan 2026-2030 due from the Ministry of Digital in the third quarter, and a digital skills subsidy launched July 28. Full implementation details, including teacher training, are still taking shape, giving schools genuine time to build AI readiness ahead of the eventual rollout.

On July 28, 2026, Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim announced that the government would subsidise AI software subscriptions for young Malaysians, the latest move in a year that has already brought two other major AI-in-education announcements. The National Education Plan 2026-2035 opened the year in January. A National AI Action Plan is due from the Ministry of Digital before the end of the third quarter.

Three initiatives in seven months signal real momentum from the federal government toward a more AI-capable education and workforce system. For school leaders, the more useful question is what each piece is actually building toward, and where schools have room to move early.

What is Malaysia's National Education Plan 2026-2035 doing for AI in schools?

The National Education Plan 2026-2035, launched in January, is the anchor document. Anwar framed it around urgency, arguing that emerging skills like AI and digital literacy "must be embraced now" rather than phased in gradually. The plan embeds AI, digital literacy, STEM and vocational training across the system, from primary school through university. A parallel Higher Education Plan launched alongside it, and vocational training is set to move into the school curriculum from Year One through Form Three starting in 2027, under separate reforms.

It builds on groundwork already in place. The Digital Education Policy from 2023 already commits schools to adopting AI-based adaptive learning tools. A National AI Office, established in December 2024, has been issuing guidelines on AI ethics and governance since before the new plan arrived. School leaders reading the plan for the first time are not looking at a standing start. They are looking at the next layer on a foundation that has been building for a couple of years.

What will the National AI Action Plan 2026-2030 bring?

The Action Plan is expected from the Ministry of Digital in the third quarter of this year. Early reporting describes a workforce-development plan built around a target of 30,000 AI professionals by 2030, aimed at the wider economy rather than at classrooms specifically. That focus matters for schools in an indirect but real way: a larger national pool of AI-skilled professionals also means more potential industry partners and career pathways schools can point students toward once the plan is published.

Worth watching closely when it lands: whether it names school teachers as a distinct group within that workforce target, since that would be the clearest signal yet of what comes next for K-12.

What does the July 2026 digital skills subsidy offer?

The subsidy programme, announced July 28, will cover AI software subscriptions for young Malaysians, with free access for three months once enrolled. Training modules, expected to span AI safety, cybersecurity, agentic AI, generative AI and cloud literacy, are due to begin August 31. The programme currently targets people aged 18 to 30, a scope the government has described as "for now," leaving room for it to widen.

It is a useful signal in its own right: a government willing to subsidise direct AI access for young people is a government building habits and expectations that schools can plan around, even before the programme's scope is finalised.

What AI teacher training is already happening in Malaysia?

Momentum is not only coming from federal announcements. Several programmes are already active and available to schools right now.

What should school leaders do with this window?

Plug into existing programmes like Experience AI or the Google.org AI Class ASEAN modules now. These are live and already adapted for the Malaysian classroom. Waiting for a national rollout to arrive means waiting to use tools that are already available.

Build a teacher AI baseline while the runway is open. A school that already knows where its teachers stand is ready to move the moment the Action Plan or further curriculum guidance arrives, instead of starting the assessment work from scratch. The TEACH-AI framework gives that baseline structure across seven domains.

Position your school as a visible early mover. Ministries building out national programmes look for pilot sites and case studies as implementation guidance takes shape. Schools with a track record in teacher AI readiness are natural candidates for that role.

Start assessment redesign work ahead of any formal mandate. Whatever shape the eventual curriculum guidance takes, the underlying task, teaching and assessing well in a classroom where students already use AI, is worth starting now rather than waiting for a ministry timeline.

Frequently asked questions

What AI-related education plans has Malaysia announced in 2026?

Three so far. The National Education Plan 2026-2035 launched in January and covers the whole education system. The National AI Action Plan 2026-2030 is expected from the Ministry of Digital in the third quarter, focused on the broader AI workforce. A digital skills subsidy for people aged 18 to 30 followed on July 28.

When will Malaysia's National AI Action Plan be released, and what will it cover?

It is expected in the third quarter of 2026. Early reporting points to a workforce-development plan targeting 30,000 AI professionals by 2030, focused on the wider economy, with the specifics for education still to be confirmed once the plan is published.

Does Malaysia have a mandatory AI curriculum for schools?

Not yet as a compulsory subject with a fixed date, the way CBSE introduced one for Classes 3 to 8 in India. Malaysia's National Education Plan 2026-2035 embeds AI, digital literacy, STEM and vocational training as system-wide priorities, and further implementation detail is expected as the plan and the Action Plan mature.

What AI training opportunities are already available to Malaysian teachers?

Several are already running. The Raspberry Pi Foundation's Experience AI programme has trained over 1,300 teachers through Penang Science Cluster, and Google.org has funded AI Class ASEAN modules translated into Bahasa Malaysia. Universiti Malaysia Sarawak has certified more than 500 of its own lecturers.

How can schools prepare while implementation details are still being finalized?

Schools do not need to wait for a national mandate to start. Building an internal picture of teacher AI readiness now, and using existing programmes like Experience AI in the meantime, means a school is ready to move quickly once further national guidance arrives.
